01

Scattering

Air scatters short wavelengths far more than long ones, blue about six times more than red. At noon the whole sky fills with blue. At sunset, sunlight crosses so much air that mostly red is left.

Rayleigh scattering ∝ 1/λ4

02

Aurora

Charged particles from the Sun follow Earth's magnetic field down into the upper atmosphere. Oxygen around 100 to 250 km up glows green; thinner oxygen higher up glows red.

Green 557.7 nm, red 630.0 nm

03

Caustics

The bright net on a pool floor is sunlight focused by the moving curves of the water's surface. Every ripple is a small, imperfect lens.

From the Greek kaustikos, burning

04

Dispersion

Glass slows violet light a little more than red, so each colour leaves a prism at its own angle. In 1666 Newton used this to show that white light is a mixture of colours.

Violet bends most, red least

05

Bioluminescence

In the deep sea, most light is made by animals: about three quarters of the creatures observed off California glow. Plankton flash when something disturbs them.

Move through them to see it

06

Lensing

Mass bends the path of light. When a distant galaxy sits exactly behind a heavy object, its light reaches us from every side at once, as a ring.

First measured in the 1919 solar eclipse
